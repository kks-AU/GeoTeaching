Modified Cam Clay

Follow a clay specimen from its initial stress state through yielding. Change its stress history or the drainage conditions to see how the stress path, yield surface and specific volume respond.

Follow the test

Each point uses the Hvorslev equivalent pressure p′e at its current specific volume. It stays constant in undrained shearing and increases as the specimen contracts.

Model equations

Stress invariants in triaxial compression

p′=σ1′+2σ3′3,q=σ1′−σ3′

Yield ellipse and critical state

q2=M2p′(pc′−p′),q=Mp′

Specific volume on the swelling line through the current yield pressure

v=N−λlnpc′pref′+κlnpc′p′

Hvorslev equivalent pressure on the normal compression line at the current specific volume v

pe′=pref′exp(N−vλ)

pc0′=OCRp0′,Δu=p0′+q3−p′

Triaxial strains, positive in compression

εv=lnv0v,ε1=εv3+εq
Symbols and assumptions

σ′1 and σ′3 are axial and radial effective stresses. p′c is the current isotropic yield pressure and p′c0 its initial value. Stresses and pore pressure are in kPa, with compression positive.

M is the critical state ratio q/p′. In the v–ln(p′) plane, λ and κ are the magnitudes of the normal compression and swelling slopes. N sets the specific volume on the normal compression line at p′ref = 1 kPa.

The specimen starts isotropic. Both triaxial tests hold total cell pressure constant. The undrained test assumes saturation and incompressible constituents. The exercise follows analytical stress paths with associated plastic flow and uses illustrative material values.

v0 is the initial specific volume. Strains are logarithmic and displayed as percentages. The deviatoric strain εq combines elastic strain from the selected shear modulus G, which stays constant during each test, with plastic strain integrated along the stress path.

The model generally approaches critical state asymptotically. To show the plateau at finite strain, the simulator switches to the theoretical limit once the stress ratio q/p′ is within 0.1% of M.

Try these comparisons

  1. Run a normally consolidated specimen in drained and undrained compression. Which way does p′ move after yielding, and what happens to v?
  2. Set OCR to 3 in the undrained test. Follow the path past first yield. Does p′c grow or shrink?
  3. Run isotropic compression and unloading with OCR above 1. Compare the swelling and normal compression slopes. Does the specimen recover its initial volume when it returns to p′0?
  4. Normalise the 2D plots and compare undrained tests at OCR = 1 and OCR = 3. Where do the paths first meet the state boundary, and where do they end?
Check your reasoning

In the drained normally consolidated test, p′ increases and v decreases. In the undrained normally consolidated test, v stays constant and p′ falls as excess pore pressure builds.

At OCR = 3 the undrained path reaches yield above the critical state line and the ellipse shrinks as the specimen approaches critical state. Isotropic unloading follows the flatter swelling line, leaving a permanent volume reduction.

At OCR = 1 the normalised path starts at p′/p′e = 1 with zero deviator stress. At OCR = 3 it first reaches the state boundary above the critical state line. With the same material parameters both paths end at the same normalised critical state point.
